Velocity profile

  • Classical, no slip (Eqs. 1–2 of the source): Hagen–Poiseuille flow, u(r) = (R²G/4μ)(1 − r²/R²), zero velocity at the boundary.
  • Slip-velocity form (Eqs. 3 and 5): the same parabola plus the slip velocity us at the boundary, u(r) = (R²G/4μ)(1 − r²/R²) + us.
  • Molecular dynamics: six points of the published profile (Fig. 6a of the source), read from the figure.

r is the distance from the tube axis, R the boundary radius, G = ΔP/L the pressure gradient (ΔP between the bulk regions of the two reservoirs, L the tube length: the source’s convention) and μ the viscosity. The profile difference is the root-mean-square of (umodel − uMD)/uMD over the molecular-dynamics points inside the boundary.

How the comparison is made

The flow-rate and velocity-profile differences carry no pass or fail. A verdict needs an accuracy requirement written down for a stated use, and none exists for this case. Both differences are reported as calculated, with the uncertainty that comes from reading the published figures (one standard uncertainty; it is not a statistical uncertainty of the simulation).

Viscosity

The default is the slip-velocity form at the carbon-centre boundary with the interfacial viscosity. It follows a rule set while the case was planned (10 November 2026): it is the one combination for which the source shows both the velocity profile (Fig. 6a) and the flow rate (Fig. 7c). The published differences of several combinations were already known then, and the default is not the combination that agrees best with the molecular-dynamics flow rate. Choosing a combination by its agreement would be tuning; the agreement table lists all twelve.

A model sensitivity, not part of the comparison. It adds a resistance cμ/R³ for the two ends in series with the tube and computes the profile with the interior pressure drop only. The source’s Eqs. 3 and 5 assign the whole pressure drop to the tube, and the source gives no value of c. See Model and equations.

Status: a published-case reconstruction, not a prediction

Model inputs include the slip velocity measured in the same driven molecular-dynamics case that supplies the reference points. The comparison therefore shows how well this model form, with these inputs, reconstructs that one published run. It does not test a prediction.

Four kinds of statement are kept apart:

  1. Numerical verification. Does the code solve the stated equations? Pass or fail.
  2. Source-curve reproduction. With the parameters read from the source paper, does the code return the model curves and flow rates that the paper plots? Pass or fail, within the extraction uncertainty.
  3. Agreement with molecular dynamics. The differences between the model and the published simulation points, reported as calculated; no pass or fail, because no accuracy requirement exists (how the comparison is made).
  4. Independent physical validation. Not established. It needs boundary inputs obtained independently of the reference run and a test against an accuracy requirement written down for a stated use, on a condition that was not used to choose those inputs. No published case provides such a condition yet.

Evidence labels on this page: Published-case reconstruction, the model inputs include values measured in the same driven run as the reference. Equilibrium-input comparison, the inputs come from equilibrium simulations and are compared with driven runs; no such case is enabled yet.

Model and equations

Flow in the tube

Steady, fully developed flow of a Newtonian liquid with one viscosity μ in a cylinder of radius R, driven by a pressure gradient G. Two forms of the source paper are enabled for this case.

ModelVelocity profileFlow rate
Classical, no slip (Eqs. 1–2)u(r) = (R²G/4μ)(1 − r²/R²)Q = πR⁴G/(8μ)
Slip-velocity form (Eqs. 3 and 5)u(r) = (R²G/4μ)(1 − r²/R²) + usQ = πR⁴G/(8μ) + πR²us

The slip velocity us is the liquid velocity at the boundary, taken from the molecular simulation. The pressure gradient is G = ΔP/L, with ΔP the difference between the bulk regions of the two reservoirs and L the tube length. That is the source paper’s convention; it assigns the whole pressure drop to the tube. The mean velocity is Q/(πR²).

Where the liquid ends: two boundary conventions

The source paper uses two continuum boundary conventions for the same molecular system: the carbon-centre boundary, R = D/2 with D the tube diameter between carbon centres, and the first-density-peak boundary, R = D′/2 with D′ the diameter of the first density peak of the water. Each comes with its own slip velocity, measured at that position in the same driven run. Changing the convention therefore changes two model inputs, the effective radius and the slip velocity; the molecular-dynamics reference does not change. Neither convention is asserted here to be the correct one. The band drawn by “Compare boundary definitions” is the sensitivity to the boundary convention, not an uncertainty or a confidence interval.

Viscosity

Three values from the source paper (Fig. 8c): the interfacial viscosity from non-equilibrium molecular dynamics (local shear stress over shear rate), the Green–Kubo viscosity from equilibrium molecular dynamics, and the experimental viscosity of water. The source also plots a bulk-liquid viscosity, which lies between the last two for this tube and is not offered separately. The model uses one viscosity across the whole section.

Equivalent slip length

The Navier condition (Eq. 8) relates the slip velocity to the wall shear rate, us = Ls |du/dr| at r = R, which gives Ls = 2μus/(GR). The page reports this length for information. The source paper’s own slip-length forms (Eqs. 4 and 6) are not enabled for this case; the case sheet says why.

Entrance-resistance sensitivity (assumed coefficient)

The source paper’s Eq. 7 puts a resistance for the two ends in series with the tube: Q = ΔP / (Rends + Rtube), with Rends = cμ/R³ and Rtube = 8μL / [π(R⁴ + 4R³Ls)]. It does not give a value for c. The sensitivity uses c = 3, Sampson’s result for a circular opening in a thin plane wall without slip, both sides together [1], and the equivalent slip length above. The profile is computed with the interior pressure drop only, ΔP Rtube/(Rends + Rtube), so no loss is counted twice.

Because it changes the model and rests on an assumed coefficient, this is reported as a model sensitivity, apart from the reproduction of the source curves and from the comparison. The value of c for the geometry of this simulation is not known.

Uncertainty

Every input was read from a published figure and carries an extraction uncertainty of 2.5 pixels on the figure axis, taken as one standard uncertainty. The uncertainty of the model flow rate propagates those of the pressure drop, the viscosity, the slip velocity and, at D′, the boundary diameter to first order. It says how well the figures could be read. It is not a statistical uncertainty or a confidence interval of the simulation.

  1. Sampson, R. A. (1891). On Stokes’s current function. Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 182, 449.
Limitations
  • One tube, one pressure drop, one temperature, one published simulation. Nothing here says how the result changes with diameter, drive or temperature.
  • The inputs were read from figures, not taken from the simulation files. Each carries an extraction uncertainty; the case sheet lists the items that the original numbers would settle.
  • The model is a continuum description with one viscosity and a sharp boundary. For water in a tube of this size both are definitions with more than one reasonable choice, which is why the page offers three viscosities and two boundary conventions and asserts none of them.
  • The slip velocity was measured in the driven run that also supplies the reference, so the comparison cannot count as a prediction.
  • End resistance is not part of the source’s Eqs. 3 and 5. The sensitivity uses an assumed coefficient.
  • The open items of the case sheet (tube length, slip velocity at the carbon-centre boundary, flow definition and others) are unresolved.
  • The page was checked in headless Chromium only; Safari, Firefox, real phones and screen readers were not tested.
