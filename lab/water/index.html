Live Water Lab

Explore how molecular forces produce collective motion. Change the conditions, gently pull a molecule, and follow the response.

Pull one molecule. Watch its neighbours respond. Release it and follow the relaxation.

216 rigid SPC/E water molecules simulated live by LAMMPS compiled to WebAssembly. The 6.4 MB engine and the starting configuration load only when you start.

Moving the handle applies an external force. Molecular interactions transmit the disturbance through the liquid. After release, the system relaxes while thermal motion continues.

Forces, work and energy

The handle moves at most 0.5 nm/ps and is reset to within 0.3 nm of the molecule's centre of mass at every command update (every 2–10 fs); between updates the separation can exceed 0.3 nm by a few per cent, so the force can slightly exceed k × 0.3 nm. The force shown is the measured one; forces are never clipped.

Pulling runs at fixed volume (NVT): starting a pull from constant pressure switches the integrator, and the switch is recorded. During pulling the temperature and the model stay fixed and the pressure is a measured diagnostic. The tether adds momentum; the drift velocity is shown and is removed only if you press the button.

    Model and validation

    Engine

    LAMMPS 22 Jul 2025 (stable, update 4), compiled to WebAssembly with Emscripten 3.1.6 as a single-threaded library with the MOLECULE, KSPACE and RIGID packages (KISS FFT). It runs in a Web Worker in chunks of 2–30 MD steps; your actions are applied between chunks. Every force, energy and pressure on this page is computed by LAMMPS; the page only draws positions and moves the tether handle. No cross-origin isolation, service worker or special server headers are needed, so the lab runs on GitHub Pages as plain files.

    Engine choice: Atomify (github.com/andeplane/atomify) runs LAMMPS through the lammps.js package (v1.5.1), a multithreaded pthreads/Kokkos build that needs SharedArrayBuffer and a cross-origin-isolation service worker. This lab needs one small water box, so a pinned single-threaded build is the simpler deployment. A WebAssembly SIMD build was also tested and gave no speed-up (0.83–1.03×), so only the baseline build is served.

    Energy convention. The potential energy is that of the water model (LJ with tail correction, real-space and PPPM Coulomb, and bond and angle terms, which are near zero under SHAKE). It excludes the tether energy, which is reported separately, and no SPC/E self-polarisation correction is added.

    Temperature. LAMMPS compute temp/com: kinetic energy without the motion of the system's centre of mass, over 6N − 3 degrees of freedom (the SHAKE constraints are removed).

    Pressure. Virial pressure including the SHAKE constraint, PPPM and LJ tail contributions, with the kinetic part from the same temperature compute. It excludes the external tether virial. LAMMPS works in atmospheres: Patm = Pbar / 1.01325.

    O–O Lennard-Jones strength

    εOO = s × εOO,reference, with the charges, σ and the rigid geometry unchanged. The change is made in LAMMPS (pair_coeff), so forces, energy, virial and the tail correction change together. At s = 1 the page shows "Reference SPC/E parameters"; any other value is a "Modified SPC/E educational model", not a validated water model. It is not a hydrogen-bond strength control: hydrogen bonding in SPC/E comes mainly from the charges, which stay fixed. The allowed values are 0.90, 0.95, 1.00, 1.05 and 1.10; the conditions at which each was actually run are listed under Validation.

    Pulling protocol

    A harmonic tether acts on the centre of mass R of the selected molecule: U = ½ k |R − X|², F = −k (R − X), where X is the handle. The force is shared over the molecule's atoms in proportion to their mass, Fi = (mi/M) F, which pulls the molecule without a direct torque; it can still rotate through its interactions. LAMMPS evaluates the tether every step (fix addforce with per-atom variables and unwrapped coordinates, defined before SHAKE and the integrator). The handle starts at R, so there is no initial force jump. It moves at most 0.5 nm/ps, and at every command update (every 2–10 fs, between simulation chunks) it is reset to within 0.3 nm of R. Between updates R keeps moving, so the separation can exceed 0.3 nm by a few per cent and the tether force can slightly exceed k × 0.3 nm (largest separations measured in check S1 below). The force is computed by LAMMPS from the actual separation and is never clipped; the page shows the measured force. Pulling runs at fixed volume (NVT), at the volume the cell has when the pull starts; with a few hundred molecules this can differ from the mean volume by 1–2 %, which the recorded switch event states. Release sets k = 0.

    Work. Each handle move at fixed coordinates adds protocol work ΔW = U(R, Xnew) − U(R, Xold); a stiffness change adds ½ (knew − kold) |R − X|², and release adds −U. The work delivered to the molecules is the protocol work minus the tether energy. Energy given to the liquid is removed by the thermostat; that exchange is tracked separately (LAMMPS ecouple).

    Momentum. The tether is an external force, so it changes the total momentum. The drift velocity of the centre of mass is measured and plotted; it is removed only when you press the button, and that removal is recorded with its kinetic energy.

    Measurements and averages

    Measurements are recorded every 10 fs. The live means restart after every change (conditions, model, start of a pull, release) and are suspended while the molecule is driven. They are descriptive: the plain mean of the samples since the last change, including the relaxation that follows it, with no correlation correction, so the page shows no uncertainty. Uncertainties come from analyze_water.py in the download, which discards an initial period after each change, uses block averaging and estimates the integrated autocorrelation time. Elapsed time is not taken as proof of equilibrium. Displacement includes ordinary thermal motion.

    Reproduce an experiment

    "Download experiment" saves the LAMMPS input with every command issued in this browser, in order, plus the starting configuration with velocities, the final configuration, the measurements, the event history, the model and units, and analyze_water.py. Run lmp -in in.water_lab.lmp with any LAMMPS that has the MOLECULE, KSPACE and RIGID packages (RIGID provides fix shake). Molecular dynamics is chaotic, so a native replay follows the browser closely for the first picoseconds and agrees statistically afterwards.

    Sampling defaults of the analysis script (discard 20 ps after each change, 5 ps blocks, at least 8 blocks, 95 % intervals) were checked against the reference runs of check 9 (216 molecules, 298.15 K): the integrated autocorrelation times are about 0.04 ps for temperature, 0.04–0.14 ps for pressure, 0.2–0.4 ps for potential energy and 0.8–1.0 ps for the NPT density, so 5 ps blocks span at least five correlation times. The defaults remain provisional for other conditions.

    Limitations

    • A few hundred molecules over a few picoseconds show molecular mechanisms and live thermodynamics, not converged bulk transport properties.
    • Small boxes give large pressure fluctuations; finite-size effects are not corrected.
    • The tether is a teaching perturbation, not a free-energy (steered MD) protocol.
    • Values of s other than 1 define an educational model, not real water.
