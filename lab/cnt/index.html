  • Water reorients at each ion. In the narrow tubes, water on either side of an ion points opposite ways: hydrogens towards Cl⁻, away from Na⁺.

What you are looking at

Each system is a periodic 2.46 nm segment of an armchair carbon nanotube holding water and one or two Na⁺–Cl⁻ pairs. The trajectories are the short ab initio runs (0.5–0.75 ps) that started the machine-learned-potential campaign of Project 2. They show local structure, hydration and thermal motion; they are too short for diffusion or transport, and no machine-learned model is involved.

Method: CP2K 2026.1 (Quickstep, GPW): PBE with D3(BJ) dispersion, DZVP-MOLOPT-GTH and DZVP-MOLOPT-SR-GTH basis sets, GTH pseudopotentials, 400 Ry cutoff (60 Ry relative), NVT at 300 K with a 0.25 fs time step. The cell is periodic in all three directions, with about 1.59 nm of vacuum between the walls of neighbouring tube images. Positions are shown every 2 fs. For display, each tube is centred, every water is made whole across the periodic boundary, and the view is orthographic. Diameters are twice the mean carbon distance from the axis over the frames shown. In 2 frames of the (11,11) run one O–H bond briefly stretches to 0.126 nm; it is shown as recorded.

How the readouts are computed

  • Distance from the axis radial distance from the line through the carbon centroid of the tube.
  • Orientation angle between the H–O–H bisector (the vector from O to the midpoint of its two H) and the tube axis; 0° points along +axis. This is a geometric orientation, not the electronic dipole, which would require the electron density.
  • Hydrogen bond O···O distance below 0.35 nm and H–O···O angle below 30°, a common geometric criterion.
  • Hydration count water oxygens within 0.32 nm of Na⁺ or 0.38 nm of Cl⁻, approximate first-shell radii from bulk solution.
  • Nearest wall carbon shortest distance to any carbon of the tube, using the periodic image along the axis.
