How to read this view

The back half of the membrane and of the nanotube is drawn; the colour is the computed field on the section through the pore axis, inside the pore and in the two access regions of the reduced model (cones of slope 4/π, which reproduce Hall’s access resistance). Drag to rotate, scroll or pinch to zoom, double-click to reset; with the view focused, arrow keys rotate.

Markers are not a recorded trajectory. They are placed with the computed line density NAc(x)A(x) and move at the mean drift speed J/(NAcA) of the computed flux, so their density and their crossing rate follow the solution; thermal motion is not drawn. One second on screen is 100 ns. At equilibrium the markers stop: the net drift is zero, while the ions keep moving thermally. The drawing follows the model step: point ions, then spheres of the crowding size, then ions with the first-shell waters the dehydration closure assumes (fewer inside the pore).

    How molecular simulation enters the prediction

    Recorded CP2K hydration structures enter an illustrative solvation closure; the browser solves the resulting continuum transport model.

    The recorded runs enter through one number per ion and tube: how many first-shell waters the ion keeps inside the tube. With the hydration free energy (Marcus) and the first-shell radius from the same runs, the closure, an explicit assumption, converts the lost waters into the free energy of entering the pore; the electrodiffusion solver turns that into current and reversal potential. {{CLOSURE_CMP}} The sensitivity ranking then names the molecular calculations that could constrain the illustrative inputs: a free-energy profile, longer sampling of the shell, a dielectric response, a diffusion coefficient, a slip length or a protonation state.

    The model ladder and the theories behind it

    Every step solves the same steady electrodiffusion equations for the same pore; each adds one ingredient from an established continuum theory of ion channels and nanopores. The excess chemical potential carries only terms that mean-field electrostatics does not: crowding and solvation. The electrostatics of the fixed and mobile charges stays in Poisson’s equation, so nothing is counted twice.

    Beyond mean field

    The model-domain flags under the results say when these effects should be expected; none is in the equations:

    • Discrete occupancy and Coulomb blockade. With fewer than one ion in the pore on average, conduction proceeds by discrete entries that may be correlated; ionic Coulomb blockade has been observed in some sub-nanometre pores {{R:feng2016}}. Low mean occupancy is a reason to examine discrete or correlated transport, not evidence that blockade occurs here.
    • Single-file knock-on. Ions in single file move in concert, as in the K⁺ channel filter {{R:kopfer2014}}; a mean-field flux cannot represent it.
    • Field-dependent conductivity. At strong fields the ionic conductivity depends on the field itself; generalized fluctuation–dissipation relations obtain it from molecular simulation {{R:lesnicki2020}}. The model uses field-independent diffusivities.
    • Pore-to-pore heterogeneity. Nominally identical sub-nanometre graphene pores differ in conductance and selectivity {{R:jain2015}}; one deterministic pore is a model of one pore.
    • Single-species selectivity. Size and charge exclusion alone rarely separate ions of the same charge; single-species selectivity needs specific interactions at the pore {{R:epsztein2020}}.
    Biological inspiration

    Biological channels select ions by replacing the water an ion loses with ligands placed at the right distance. The lab borrows that idea as an illustrative parameter, the fraction κ of the lost first-shell energy that the pore wall gives back, with two illustrative lining motifs inspired by coordination chemistry:

    • Carbonyl cages (K⁺ channels). In the KcsA selectivity filter, backbone carbonyl oxygens form cages that take the place of the hydration shell around a dehydrated K⁺ {{R:doyle1998}}{{R:zhou2001}}. The carbonyl-lined motif gives Na⁺ a higher κ than Cl⁻.
    • Hydrogen-bond donors (CLC). In CLC chloride channels and transporters, Cl⁻ is coordinated by backbone amide N–H groups and side-chain hydroxyls {{R:dutzler2002}}. The donor-lined motif gives Cl⁻ the higher κ.
    • Charged rings. Rings of charged residues at the entrance of the acetylcholine receptor channel set its conductance {{R:imoto1988}}: step 2 of the ladder.
    • Engineered analogue. Short carbon nanotubes inserted in lipid membranes act as channels, carbon-nanotube porins {{R:tunuguntla2017}}.

    The experiment is a bioinspired synthetic pore, not a simulation of a protein channel and not a demonstration of K⁺/Na⁺ selectivity. The κ values are stated examples: neither the short CP2K hydration counts nor the KcsA and CLC structures calibrate them for this CNT/NaCl system. K⁺/Na⁺ selectivity by coordination and mechanosensitive gating are later modules; they need models and evidence this release does not have.

    Electrodiffusion along the pore axis

    Area-averaged equations for Na⁺ and Cl⁻ in a cylindrical pore of radius a and length L = 5.00 nm, with x along the axis from the feed (left) to the permeate (right). The fixed charge of the rims (step 2) is spread over 0.5 nm at each mouth. Reservoirs: concentration and potential fixed 300 nm beyond each mouth.

    Excess chemical potential (no mean-field electrostatics)

    {{EQ_MUEX}}
    {{EQ_BORN}}
    {{EQ_DEHYD}}

    s(x) is a smooth step of width 0.15 nm at each mouth. Born radii aB are chosen so that the Born equation reproduces the hydration Gibbs energies of Marcus (−365 kJ mol⁻¹ for Na⁺, −340 kJ mol⁻¹ for Cl⁻) {{R:marcus1991}}: 0.188 nm and 0.202 nm. ΔG₁ is the part of the hydration energy carried by the first shell: the total minus the Born energy of a sphere of radius r₁ + rw (r₁ = 0.24 nm for Na⁺ and 0.32 nm for Cl⁻ from the recorded CP2K run, rw = 0.14 nm): 185 and 191 kJ mol⁻¹. Nlost is the reference shell (the CP2K (11,11) run) minus the shell in the selected tube.

    Access regions

    {{EQ_ACCESS}}

    Outside the pore the cross-section grows as a cone of slope 4/π, which gives exactly Hall’s access resistance {{R:hall1975}} in the Ohmic limit (test I3), and, with a factor 9/4, Sampson’s viscous resistance of an orifice {{R:sampson1891}}.

    Electro-osmotic flow

    {{EQ_FLOW}}

    The body force is the force on the ions, electric and excess; at equilibrium it is balanced by the osmotic pressure, so no water moves (test I1), an ion-excluding pore under a salt gradient gives van ’t Hoff osmosis (I11c), and the coupling coefficients obey Onsager reciprocity (I11b). Slip only in the pore; no slip in the access regions.

    Reversal potential and GHK-equivalent selectivity

    {{EQ_GHK}}

    The reversal potential Vrev is the direct output: the zero-current potential with {{C_FEED}} on the feed side and one tenth of it on the permeate side (the current–voltage curve uses equal reservoirs). The GHK-equivalent Na⁺/Cl⁻ selectivity PNa/PCl is the permeability ratio with which the constant-field Goldman–Hodgkin–Katz equation reproduces Vrev, using reservoir activities when the finite-size term is on. In a charged pore with access regions and excess free energies this is an effective, model-dependent descriptor, not a measured permeability, also without water flow; the constant-field interpretation has known limits when the Debye length is much smaller than the channel {{R:green2025}}. Ratios above 10⁴ are shown as “> 10⁴” and below 10⁻⁴ as “< 10⁻⁴”; within 1 nV of a Nernst potential of the reservoirs the ratio is reported as that limit; a Vrev beyond a Nernst potential has no positive GHK ratio and is shown as “outside GHK range” (test I15). With electro-osmosis the water flow also carries ions at zero current.

    Discretisation and solver

    Finite volumes on {{NODES}} nodes: 0.0125 nm cells within 0.6 nm of each mouth, 0.025 nm in the rest of the pore, and cells growing by a factor 1.08 out to the reservoirs. Scharfetter–Gummel fluxes {{R:scharfetter1969}} in Slotboom form: the unknowns are the potential and, for each ion, u = c eψ − χ relative to its reservoir value, where χ = Q∫dx/(DA) accounts for the water flow. An ion that carries no net flux has constant u, so the small flux of an excluded ion is computed without cancellation (test I5). Newton’s method on a block-tridiagonal system bordered by the flow unknown, continuation in the drives, Float64, in a Web Worker. Each run solves all six steps at the set voltage, the selected model and the classical reference at 17 voltages, two reversal potentials and each illustrative input at both ends of its range: about 70 Newton solves. In headless Chromium on the build machine (two cores at 2.8 GHz) the first result appears after about 0.6 s and the whole run takes about 2 s.

    Evidence: what is measured and what is not

    Inputs carry one of five statuses. Literature: a published measurement or reference correlation. Recorded (CP2K): read from this site’s CP2K trajectories, with the caveats below. Derived: computed from the entries above by a stated formula. Illustrative: a stated example, not the result of an assessed calculation. Setting: chosen by the visitor. No input is obtained from an assessed molecular calculation, so none is labelled “MD-informed”.

    Reproduce and download

    “Download result” saves a JSON file with the inputs, the results at the set voltage, both current–voltage curves, the reversal potentials, the ranking of illustrative inputs, the evidence table and the SHA-256 hashes of the solver files that produced it; “Profiles” saves the axial profiles as CSV. node tools/continuum/ions/reproduce_ions.js result.json recomputes the result from the inputs with the same solver files and checks it (relative tolerance 10⁻¹²).

    The solver tests (node tools/continuum/ions/t_ions.js), the independent SciPy reference (tools/continuum/ions/ref_pnp.py), the generator of the hydration counts (tools/continuum/ions/make_hydration.py) and the frozen acceptance criteria (tools/continuum/ions/TOLERANCES.md) are in the site’s repository.

    Limitations
    • Mean-field continuum: in sub-nanometre pores at these concentrations there is usually less than one ion in the pore, and the result is a time average over discrete events (flag “occupancy”).
    • The (6,6) tube holds a single file of water: the continuum description is outside its domain there, and the result is shown for comparison only.
    • One-dimensional and area-averaged: the radial structure of the double layer is not resolved; the access cones reproduce Hall’s and Sampson’s resistances, not the three-dimensional field near the mouth.
    • Except for the tube radii, the first-shell radii and the first-shell counts, every molecular input is a literature value or a stated example. The counts come from runs of 0.50–0.75 ps and are not converged. The solvation closure is an assumption: the difference between closures is a sensitivity of the prediction, and the short AIMD trajectories do not establish the transport barrier or the current.
    • One permittivity everywhere in Poisson’s equation; dielectric effects enter only through the Born closure. No charge regulation, ion pairing or specific adsorption.
    • Electro-osmosis: area-averaged flow with the force spread over the cross-section and slip only in the pore.
    • NaCl only; no K⁺, no divalent ions.
    • The membrane estimate assumes independent pores at one per 100 nm², all at the same voltage: no shared access resistance or concentration polarisation.
