    How the scales connect
    {{DIAGRAM}}

    Three descriptions are linked, each at its own scale and with its own source:

    1. One tube (molecular). A pore law gives the flow q through one (6,6) tube for a driving potential ΔΠ across it. In this release the law is a declared input scenario; the calibration package below defines the molecular dynamics that would replace it. The recorded CP2K trajectory shows the tube’s structure; it does not supply the law.
    2. Membrane map. The pores sit on a square lattice of 10 nm pitch. Each continuum face of the membrane (100 nm × 100 nm on the standard mesh of the default device) aggregates the tubes whose centres it contains into a conductance sum S_c = Σ G_i, counted exactly. A change of one tube changes one term of one sum.
    3. Chambers (continuum). Steady Stokes flow in the feed and permeate chambers is solved in 3D. Each open face couples the two chambers through J_c = ΔΠ_c / Rs_c with Rs_c = A_c / S_c, inside the same linear solve; blocked faces carry J = 0 exactly.

    What passes between them: the pore law and the pore count pass upward as S_c; the solved field passes downward as the local ΔΠ_c of every face, from which the page reports q_i = G_i ΔΠ_c for the selected tube. The camera move between views is a visual explanation: a molecular frame does not become a continuum field. Scale bars, source labels and clocks stay separate: the recorded trajectory has a time axis in picoseconds; the continuum solution is steady and has none.

    Model and equations

    Chambers

    {{EQ_STOKES}}

    Water at 300 K: ρ = 996.557 kg m⁻³, μ = 853.742 µPa s (NIST, IAPWS-95 and Huber et al. 2009). Each chamber is W × B × H with H = min(W, B)/2; square ports of side min(W, B)/5 sit at the centres of the top (feed) and bottom (permeate) faces. Walls are no-slip. Pressures are outlet-referenced: the permeate port face is at 0.101325 MPa and the feed port face at 0.101325 MPa + ΔP. The density change over 0–10 MPa is 0.446 %, inside the 1 % incompressibility gate. A port Reynolds number above 0.1 is reported as outside the creeping-flow scope.

    Membrane interface

    {{EQ_INTERFACE}}

    Feed and permeate faces share one flux unknown per open membrane face, so interface conservation is exact. The macroscopic tangential velocity is zero on both membrane faces (declared closure for an impermeable matrix with aligned pores). With that closure ∂w/∂z = 0 at the faces, so the normal traction difference equals the pressure difference across each face; the driving potential is defined on these membrane-face planes (reference-plane convention “membrane-faces-normal-traction”).

    {{EQ_PORELAW}}
    {{EQ_SCOPE}}

    Selected tube, selected patch and whole membrane each multiply a different factor: F_i for one tube, F_patch for the faces of one patch, F_membrane for all. A factor of zero closes the scope; a face whose sum S_c becomes zero is a Dirichlet condition J_c = 0, not a large resistance.

    Discretisation and solver

    Staggered (MAC) finite volumes on uniform cubic cells, rows scaled by control volume so the coupled matrix is symmetric, K = [A, Gᵀ; G, 0]. Lengths are scaled by min(W, B), velocities by U = 1 Pa / (Rs,ref + μ/L), and each chamber carries its pressure as a gauge relative to its own port, so the 0.1–10 MPa levels never enter the unknowns. MINRES with a block-diagonal preconditioner (symmetric Gauss–Seidel on velocities), Float64, restarted until ‖b − Kx‖/‖b‖ ≤ 10⁻¹⁰. Changes of the membrane only are solved as changes of a stored state: the difference K₁Δx = d is assembled from the change itself, which keeps a one-tube change exact even in a 10¹⁰-pore membrane.

    Molecular observable for calibration

    {{EQ_CROSS}}

    Net crossings of one tube in time Δt, converted to an equivalent volume flow with the stated reference density ρ_ref = 996.557 kg m⁻³ and m_w = 2.991506 × 10⁻²⁶ kg (18.015268 g mol⁻¹ / N_A). The mass flow is the conserved molecular quantity when the local density varies.

    Pore law, provenance and the missing measurement
    Lawillustrative-cnt66-v1: q = G ΔΠ, G = 1.00 × 10⁻²⁷ m³ Pa⁻¹ s⁻¹ (3.33 water molecules per ns at 100 MPa)
    Order of magnitudeFor scale only: G corresponds to an osmotic permeability p_f = G k_B T / v_w = 1.38 × 10⁻¹³ cm³ s⁻¹ per pore (v_w = m_w / ρ_ref = 3.00 × 10⁻²⁹ m³, 300 K), about twice the simulated value for aquaporin-1, 7.1 × 10⁻¹⁴ cm³ s⁻¹ (Zhu, Tajkhorshid and Schulten, Biophys. J. 86, 50, 2004); molecular dynamics of carbon-nanotube membranes gives water fluxes per tube comparable to aquaporin-1 (Kalra, Garde and Hummer, PNAS 100, 10175, 2003). This places the declared value in a plausible range; it is not a calibration.
    Source kind / assessmentdeclared_scenario / unassessed. A declared value is an input scenario; it is not measured water/CNT performance and carries no uncertainty.
    DomainWater, 300 K, (6,6) geometry, tube length 2.4595 nm only, pitch 10 nm, 0 ≤ ΔΠ ≤ 10 MPa on the membrane-face planes

    The smallest measurement that would replace the illustrative law

    Net water crossings through the declared (6,6) membrane geometry with reservoirs on both sides (pure SPC/E water, 300 K, fixed mean pressure), at three or more drives of both signs plus zero, with at least four independent replicas per drive (eight at zero drive) and crossing counts at two planes. The input templates, the manifest and the analysis program (analyze_pore_flux.py, with block averaging, bootstrap intervals, intercept and symmetry tests and held-out drives) are in tools/continuum/md-calibration/. Molecular dynamics resolves the flow only at 50–200 MPa, so the program lets a law cover this lab’s 0–10 MPa only if the equilibrium fluctuations of the zero-drive runs give the same conductance as the driven slope (fluctuation–dissipation check); otherwise the law is limited to the tested drives. A qualified law would carry its own uncertainty; one shared conductance sample then applies to every equivalent pore, so the relative uncertainty of Q does not shrink with the number of pores.

    Reproduce and download

    “Download experiment” saves a zip with the inputs (scenario.json), the pore law that drove the CFD (pore_law.json), the results with residuals, conservation, scope and decision (results.json), the 3D fields as VTK image data for ParaView (fields/*.vti), the per-face membrane table (membrane.csv), probes, the ordered input events with revision numbers, the environment with SHA-256 hashes of the solver files, and a README. node reproduce.js in the unzipped folder recomputes the experiment with the same solver files and checks Q against the export (relative tolerance 10⁻⁹); with a baseline it also recomputes the change.

    Native reference cases for OpenFOAM v1912 (developed duct, uniform porous baffle, closed strip, refined mesh, and an 80-cell closed-strip case for the convergence study) and the comparison program are in tools/continuum/openfoam/; their measured agreement is in the validation table.

    Limitations
    • The pore law is illustrative. Totals, fluxes and the single-pore flow follow from it exactly, but none of them is a prediction for water in real (6,6) nanotubes until a qualified law replaces it.
    • One geometry, one length, one temperature and pure water; no salt, osmosis, heat transport, deformable tubes or entrance-model choice.
    • Steady creeping flow; ports and chambers are idealised boxes. Above the Reynolds-number gate the page keeps the last valid result.
    • The standard mesh resolves the device-level flow (mesh checks in the decision panel); it does not resolve the flow near individual pores, which the pore law represents.
      • {{LIMIT_PORTS}}
    • Pore-to-pore variability and conductance uncertainty are not modelled; a scoped change is a deterministic what-if.
    • Neighbouring tubes interact only hydraulically, through the chamber water. Molecular interactions between tubes (for example carbon polarization or the influence of adjacent tubes on the confined water) are not modelled; they would enter through each tube’s conductance G_i.
