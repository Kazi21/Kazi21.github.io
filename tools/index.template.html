Project 1

Molecular structure, boundary conditions and nanoscale transport

Published research

Scientific question
Where does a confined fluid actually end at a nanoscale wall, and which continuum quantities, boundary conditions and transport laws still describe what the molecules do there?
My contribution
A first-law energy-balance definition of the flow boundary inside a carbon nanotube, tested against Hagen–Poiseuille limits (Phys. Rev. E 2021), and a critical review with scale-effects analysis of water-transport estimates in nanotubes (Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys. 2024).
Available evidence
Phys. Rev. E 103, 053115 (2021) · Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys. 26, 19069 (2024)
Methods and next development

In Physical Review E (2021) I developed a first-law energy-balance analysis that locates the flow boundary for a simple liquid inside a single-walled carbon nanotube from non-equilibrium and equilibrium molecular dynamics, and tested the resulting boundary against Hagen–Poiseuille limits under controlled wall–fluid interaction. In Physical Chemistry Chemical Physics (2024) I led a critical review of experimental and simulation estimates of water transport in carbon nanotubes and a scale-effects analysis of how boundary definitions enter Hagen–Poiseuille-type estimates.

Next development. Ongoing work on interfacial friction and slip, molecular accessibility, finite-time diffusivity and hydraulic resistance in nanotubes and graphene nanoslits, described here as current interests; pending results are not published on this site.

Project 2

Error-aware MLIPs for CNT-confined NaCl–water

Current research

Scientific question
Which model errors matter for the intended transport prediction, and what should be calculated next?
My contribution
An active CP2K–DP-GEN–DeePMD–LAMMPS workflow whose emphasis is error-aware validation: reference-protocol checks, per-species force errors, committee uncertainty checked against true error, and a rules-based decision on the next calculation.
Available evidence
Methods and software demonstration with synthetic example data: three inspectable entry points, tests executed, source and download. No campaign numbers, models or trajectories are published.
Methods and next development

Periodic Gaussian-and-plane-wave reference calculations in CP2K (PBE-D3(BJ), GTH pseudopotentials, DZVP-MOLOPT basis sets, with the Fermi-smearing SCF strategy that metallic armchair tubes require); selection of reference configurations by DP-GEN active learning with a DeePMD-kit committee; consistency checks on the reference data; element-resolved force errors (Na⁺, Cl⁻, O, H) with committee disagreement; structural diagnostics, stability testing and LAMMPS deployment within a declared scope. The model is energy-and-force only and local; its validity envelope is stated in the package's model-scope note.

Next development. A reproducible reference example with a real CP2K input, then a controlled test of whether targeted reference calculations improve performance on independent evaluation cases; the package's scoped next experiment is stress labels with a virial loss term behind a composition-disjoint validation set.

Project 3

Physics-informed structure–friction–flow closure

Current research

Scientific question
Can a physically constrained model connect molecular structure, interfacial friction and flow into a transport closure that is useful, and inspectable, within a clearly defined physical regime?
My contribution
Physics-informed neural-network models in which physical constraints, not only data, shape the mapping from confined molecular structure to friction and flow; the current implementation is in development and no benchmark result is published.
Available evidence
Project scope in the public CV; the molecular data come from the transport work of Project 1. A public script or benchmark will be linked only once it exists and has been inspected.
Methods and next development

The aim is a closure whose useful range and failure modes can be examined, rather than a fit that reproduces simulation data. Physics enters through the constraints imposed on the model, and the evaluation design separates training, validation and untouched evaluation conditions so that reported performance is not selected on the data that reports it.

Next development. A benchmark comparing a conventional closure, a data-driven baseline and the physics-informed model on held-out simulation conditions, with explicit checks on physical consistency and predictive uncertainty.

Can a simulation identify what to compute next?

My longer-term goal is adaptive simulation that uses physical diagnostics, model errors and uncertainty to guide where computational effort is most useful.

Research direction

The proposed cycle is deliberately plain: examine an error in the current prediction; investigate its source, whether reference data, the interaction model, sampling or the continuum description; select the one calculation most likely to resolve it; update the model; and evaluate the outcome on cases that were not used to make the decision. The first milestone is one bounded, human-reviewed decision cycle with its inputs, rationale, cost and outcome recorded. This is a research direction; no adaptive system is claimed to exist.

  1. Examine an error
  2. Investigate its source
  3. Select a useful calculation
  4. Update the model
  5. Evaluate the outcome

The decision layer allocates the physics; it does not replace it.

Research in Practice

Explore the proposed workflow. This walkthrough explains the method behind Project 2; it does not report executed training or scientific outcomes. Select a step to read its purpose. Steps 2–4 link to the methods and software demonstration (synthetic example data).

Workflow steps

Define the target

Name the transport quantity the prediction is for (flux, selectivity, friction, diffusivity), the uncertainty it must reach and the computational budget. Everything downstream is judged against this target, not against generic accuracy.

Check the reference

Verify that the CP2K reference data are internally consistent: functional and basis settings, energy, force and virial conventions, cell and cutoff choices, and whether independent evaluation configurations exist. A model trained on inconsistent references inherits the inconsistency. Entry point: CP2K protocol parsing and compatibility checking (source).

Expose model errors

Resolve the potential's errors by species and environment (Na⁺, Cl⁻, O, H; bulk-like against wall-adjacent water), compare committee members, and test structural diagnostics and MD stability. Ask which of these errors can change the target quantity. Entry point: per-species force diagnostics and uncertainty tooling (source).

Choose the next calculation

Decide between adding reference calculations where the important errors live, extending sampling where statistics limit the target, or testing the transport closure. Record the reason, run it, and return to the reference check. Entry point: the transparent decision prototype (source).

Simulation Lab

Watch a molecular model learn.

I am developing a browser experiment that connects water dynamics, force-prediction errors and targeted learning updates. The planned interface will let visitors explore molecular motion and inspect why the next calculation is chosen.

Interactive experiment in development

  1. Reference water a rigid SPC/E water box provides the trajectory and the truth for comparison.
  2. Learner a compact energy-based model is evaluated on configurations the reference produced, while the reference continues undisturbed.
  3. Error check force errors are resolved by atom and environment against physical sanity checks.
  4. Decision the panel shows why the next update or the next sample was chosen, or why it stopped.
How the planned experiment works

Reference model: rigid SPC/E water (O–H 0.1000 nm, H–O–H 109.47°), 216 molecules, damped shifted-force electrostatics with a 0.90 nm cutoff (labelled SPC/E–DSF), RATTLE constraints, velocity Verlet, periodic box of 1.862 nm; eight independently equilibrated starting states. Reported quantity, oxygen sites only, at a registered lag τ:

Sampling precision is judged by a replica-level interval evaluated at preregistered checkpoints and corrected for the number of looks:

A run ends in one of four states: precision reached, budget exhausted, invalid physics, or execution failure, and every run produces a cost-and-evidence certificate. Reaching the sampling-precision target says nothing about the learner; the learner is accepted only on its own error and physics checks against configurations it never trained on. The exercise demonstrates learning against this reference model, not quantum accuracy or a measured speed-up. Playback speed will be a display setting, separate from the integration timestep; temperature or molecule-count controls will appear only when they change the running experiment. Additional research direction: nature-inspired transport strategies (proposed)

Can transport strategies evolved by living systems become testable physical design rules for nanoscale devices? Each cue below is mapped natural mechanism → design variable → simulation experiment → engineering objective. Proposed programme: none of these is a validated device prediction. Hover or focus a cue to see which capability it targets; open it for the mapping.

Nature's transport strategies

Mangrove rootssalt exclusion under suction
Natural mechanism
Root membranes pass water and exclude most ions while transpiration pulls the flow.
Design variable
Pore radius relative to hydrated-ion radius; dehydration free-energy barrier; surface charge and Debye-length ratio.
Simulation experiment
NEMD of NaCl solutions through sub-nanometre nanotubes and slits; ion-entry potentials of mean force; water flux against rejection.
Engineering objective
Ionic selectivity at a stated water permeability: the selectivity–permeability trade-off.
Plant transpirationevaporation-driven passive flow
Natural mechanism
Evaporation at nanoscale menisci creates capillary suction that drives flow without a pump and carries latent heat away.
Design variable
Meniscus curvature and pore radius (capillary pressure); evaporative flux against the hydraulic resistance of the network; contact angle.
Simulation experiment
NEMD of evaporation-driven flow in nanochannels with liquid–vapour interfaces; interfacial heat flux.
Engineering objective
Passive pumping flux and evaporative heat removal per unit area at zero applied pressure.
Electric eelstacked ion-selective cells
Natural mechanism
Thousands of series-stacked electrocytes convert ionic concentration gradients through selective channels into a large voltage.
Design variable
Membrane cation/anion selectivity (transference number), stack count, gradient magnitude, ionic conductance.
Simulation experiment
Ionic currents and selectivity in charged nanopores under concentration and potential gradients; molecular closures feeding a Nernst–Planck stack model.
Engineering objective
Open-circuit voltage and power density from salinity gradients; voltage-controlled transport.
Moth eyegraded interfaces, no abrupt mismatch
Natural mechanism
A sub-wavelength graded surface removes the abrupt impedance change that causes reflection. The transport analogue is the entrance and exit resistance of an abrupt channel mouth.
Design variable
Gradient length of wall–fluid interaction or wettability at the entrance relative to channel radius; entrance-resistance coefficient.
Simulation experiment
NEMD flow through pores with graded entrance interactions; entrance and exit hydraulic resistance against gradient length.
Engineering objective
Reduced interfacial resistance through graded interfaces. The optical origin is the analogy only; no photonic simulation is claimed.
